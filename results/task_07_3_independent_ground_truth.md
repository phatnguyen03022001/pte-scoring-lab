Published phonetic transcriptions may describe how a phone sounds but do not automatically classify **pronunciation correctness**; target/observed inventories and legitimate allophones require independent phonetic adjudication. Forced-alignment timestamps, CEFR ratings, accent-strength labels and synthetic labels cannot replace human phone-error labels.

For Disney–Le, the study's sixteen learners made different naturalistic presentations; speaker IDs, audio hashes, transcript/prompt identities, token timestamps and training-set exposure are not released in the public article. Public-model exposure is **unknown**, and a one-annotator manual judgment does not demonstrate inter-rater agreement. Do not infer that a future speaker-disjoint split is automatically prompt-disjoint or that shared event/topic independence is proven. Before examining final-test labels, freeze audio hashes, target tokens, legal provenance, scorer identities, allowed variants, denominators, abstentions and split policy. The paper can, at best, provide a limited /s/-/z/ omission endpoint.

## Local inventory and provenance/exposure audit

- `l2-arctic-v5.0` (about 15 GB): the four Vietnamese-L1 English speakers and manual phone-error annotations; project TASK-07 results already use these speakers.
- `speechocean762` (about 1.2 GB) and `speechocean762-reconstruction` (about 232 MB): Mandarin-L1 English, human expert score artifacts and reconstruction/provenance material. The GOPT checkpoint’s checkpoint-selection procedure used SpeechOcean762 test metrics. TASK-06/TASK-07 reports and cached outputs also use it. This is not a blind comparison set.
- `epadb-quality-08` (about 27 MB) and `quality-09-epadb-new-speakers` (about 31 MB): Argentinian Spanish-L1 English with phone labels. Q08 development selected the operating point; Q09 was used as a retrospective cross-speaker diagnostic and subsequent work exposed its results. The local manifests already contain source IDs and checksums.
- `sandi-corpus-2025` (about 241 MB plus a 49 MB archive): human holistic speaking levels and text/error correction subset; no phone-error annotations. Exact item-level exposure was not audited.
- `pearson-pte-academic-2025` (about 295 MB): public preparation/sample resources, including sample prompts/responses and score guides. They are not verified independent PTE-scored learner-phone records. Local manifest expressly treats them as private reference and does not infer a Pearson reuse or AI-training grant.

The GOPT output provenance recorded in `results/gopt_speechocean762.json` says it evaluates the authors’ precomputed GOP features and the checkpoint was selected on test-set phone MSE; `results/SOURCES.md` pins the source repository revision. TASK-07 further documents the LibriSpeech-trained pipeline/checkpoint and cached SpeechOcean artifacts. Therefore neither SpeechOcean test rows nor L2-ARCTIC rows can be declared uncontaminated simply because this task would create new IDs: the former is directly checkpoint-exposed, and the latter has already been analyzed in this repository. EpaDB is also exposed as above. No external MRPL2 speaker, prompt, or audio hashes were obtainable from public evidence, so same-speaker, repeated-prompt, and duplicate-audio contamination against training/model provenance cannot be checked. A blind designation would be unsupported.

The MRPL2 README says its prompts are curated CMU ARCTIC text. That creates a **prompt-overlap risk** with L2-ARCTIC even if speaker recordings are independent. A cleared MRPL2 subset would need speaker IDs, prompt IDs/text, source hashes, and overlap review against exposed L2-ARCTIC prompts plus model-training data before freezing a blind test. No statement is made that an overlap exists or does not exist.
